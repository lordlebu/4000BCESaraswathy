**Title:** South of Tethys is playable: a slow walk through Jambhudweepa

South of Tethys is out in the browser today, free, as version 0.1.

It is a small window onto a large world. For some years I have been building Jambhudweepa: an
invented ancient South Asia with its own rivers, peoples, animals and a long history before and
after the part you can walk. The game shows one era of it, from the ground.

You play Varuna, a naturalist. You walk four countries — the delta at Lothal, the Narmada plateau,
the cold desert round the harbour at Dwarka, and the Aravali crossing — and you look at things.
What is out as you pass goes into your collection with its own written entry. There are more than
340 animals and plants, each placed where it would live. The people you meet know their ground
better than you do, and listening to them is how you find out where to go.

There is no combat and no timer. A day passes as you walk, and at night you need a roof, a camp or
your bedroll. On three of the maps you can settle, which you earn by listening and by being vouched
for, and then build in three stages.

It is made for people who like to read a world in layers and come back to it. If you want action,
this is not that.

**What is in this version**

- Four field maps, each generated from a seed you can share as a link
- A collection of what you have met, and a field diary you can save as text or a picture
- Gathering, making and cooking from what the land gives
- Three homesteads to settle
- Keyboard, mouse and touch
