# Making notes

The authored notes on every material, item, process, recipe, vehicle and homestead, kept when the
making layer moved from canon (`SouthOfTethys`) to the game on 2 October 2026. Nothing reads this file:
it is the writing, kept so none of it was lost in the move. The data the game plays from is
`data/making/crafting.json` and `data/making/homesteads.json`, which never carried most of these notes.

## Materials

### Aconite root

`material_aconite_root`

The most dangerous thing in canon's ground, and the only poison it records being used as a weapon -- on arrows, by hill people, once.

### Aero-mangrove bark

`material_aero_bark`

Stripped from the trees that hold the island edges together. Cures to a board that resists splitting along the grain the way ordinary timber does not, which is the reason the trestles are built of it.

### Ammonite shell

`material_ammonite_shell`

A fossil, weathered out of the rock: canon inlays the Board of the Chess of Fate with fossilised ammonites, so this is a material with a reputation before it is a material with a use. The black ones are not worked by anybody sensible. Taken from the ground, not from the living ammonites of the lava fields -- it was won from them until 1 October 2026, which made a fossil that never renews into something you took off an animal, and left Sura teaching a pendant on Dwarka from a shell almost nobody ever saw.

### Ashwagandha root

`material_ashwagandha_root`

Dug in the dry months and dried further. Smells faintly of a horse, which everybody mentions.

### Bamboo cane

`material_bamboo_cane`

Two classes, honestly. A whole cane is a scaffold pole; a split one is a strap. Canon records the grove for scaffolding, rafts and tools, which is all three uses of one plant.

### Bast

`material_bark_bast`

The inner bark, stripped in long ribbons and beaten soft. Taken from a tree the taker intends to keep, so it is cut in bands and never a ring.

### Basalt

`material_basalt`

The Deccan is made of it. Too tough to knap and perfect for anything that must grind -- querns, mortars, and the Board of the Chess of Fate.

### Beedu bladder oil

`material_beedu_bladder_oil`

Drawn from the gas bladders that hold a beedu up over the dunes. Thin, almost colourless, and it burns with very little smoke -- which is why a desert crossing carries it and a river settlement, with mahua oil to hand, does not bother.

### Bitter greens

`material_bitter_greens`

Fourteen leaves, and the number is the point rather than the plants. Gathered on one morning from ditch, bund, pond and field edge, most of them things nobody planted. Bitter to the last, on purpose.

### Boar tusk

`material_boar_tusk`

Curved, hollow at the root, and hard enough to take an edge. Worn as often as it is worked.

### Brahmi

`material_brahmi_leaf`

Pulled by the handful from wet mud and used fresh, which limits it to where it grows.

### Bronze

`material_bronze`

Copper and tin, and the era's outer edge. Canon is a bronze world with iron as a rumour; nothing here should let a recipe quietly invent a smelted iron industry. Cast, never found. Copper and tin and a furnace -- see recipe_cast_bronze.

### Cardamom

`material_cardamom_pod`

Three pods will scent a whole pot, which is the only reason something growing in wet shade on one plateau is eaten a thousand miles away.

### Charcoal

`material_charcoal`

Canon names the ghost mangrove for charcoal specifically. Burnt slow under turf, and the only fuel in this list hot enough to melt anything. Burnt slow under turf from timber. Not a thing lying about.

### Crayfish carapace

`material_crayfish_carapace`

Thin, blue, and worth keeping only for the colour. Ground into a pigment more often than it is worked whole.

### Croc hide

`material_croc_hide`

Scuted, awkward to cut and nearly impossible to wear out. The dromedary-croc's is the lighter of the two and the one the delta actually uses.

### Dates

`material_date_fruit`

Dried on the frond and carried in a pressed block. Sweet, heavy, and the reason a desert crossing is arithmetic rather than a gamble.

### Dhatura seed

`material_datura_seed`

Kept for the shrine and never for the pot. Canon records no preparation that makes these safe, and the households that grow it do not look for one.

### Antler

`material_deer_antler`

Shed each year and gathered off the ground, which makes it the one bone in this list that costs nothing but the walking.

### Deer hide

`material_deer_hide`

The painted deer already gave canon its antler and its sinew, and an animal that gives both gives its skin. Lighter than goat hide and the delta's ordinary leather, which is why a bedroll on Lothal is backed with it.

### Red delta rice

`material_delta_rice`

Canon records it as a staple grain and a trade good. Stores through a season, which is the whole difference between a camp and a settlement.

### Dung cake

`material_dung_cake`

Patted flat and dried on a wall. Unglamorous, universal, and what almost every meal in this world is actually cooked over.

### Fish bone

`material_fish_bone`

Every fish has bones, and a fisher keeps the big ones. The best are the rostral teeth of the sawfish, which come out of the estuary already barbed -- a harpoon made from one is a harpoon somebody did very little to -- but a river bronze's spine points a harpoon too. Was sawfish bone alone, which made it a rare animal's leavings on a coast full of fish.

### Flint

`material_flint`

Nodules out of limestone, or cobbles out of a riverbed. Knaps predictably, which is the entire reason it outlasted better-looking stone.

### Frankincense

`material_frankincense`

Canon already records the scrub for incense and trade resin. Tapped by scoring the bark and coming back a fortnight later, which makes it a crop as much as a gathering.

### Ginger

`material_ginger_root`

Dug rather than picked. Keeps a season in dry sand and travels as well as anything that is not a stone, which is most of why it is worth as much away from where it grows.

### Cliff-goat hair

`material_goat_hair`

Combed from the winter coat rather than shorn. Spins into a yarn that stays warm wet, which matters more on the Vindhya than anything else about it.

### Goat hide

`material_goat_hide`

Tanned with bark liquor. Becomes a water-skin, a drum head, or the seat of a saddle, in roughly that order of frequency.

### Sky-balloon shell

`material_gourd_shell`

The gourd is lighter than the air it displaces while it is on the vine, and merely very light once it is cut. Halved and dried it is a float, a bailer, or the body of a small drum.

### Guggul

`material_guggul_resin`

Tapped in beads from a cut in the bark. Smells like a temple and is swallowed like a pill.

### Snow hare pelt

`material_hare_pelt`

Thick white winter fur off the glacial snow hare. Too small and too thin-skinned to make a garment alone; stitched as a lining, and the warmest thing a traveller on the ice can carry.

### Haritaki

`material_haritaki_fruit`

Dried until it rattles. Powdered it is a remedy and steeped it is the tannin that blacks a hide, and only the second of those is beyond dispute.

### Indigo

`material_indigo_dye`

Fermented in a vat until it stinks, then oxidised in the air. The cloth comes out green and turns blue while you watch, which is why it is worth what it is worth.

### Jackfruit

`material_jackfruit_flesh`

Unripe it is cooked like meat and ripe it is eaten like sweets. Canon files it as produce both times, because it is a fruit whichever way it is used.

### Kuchla seed

`material_kuchla_seed`

Flat grey discs, kept in a sealed jar and counted. Nothing is made of them but medicine, and that only after a fortnight of soaking.

### Lamp black

`material_lamp_black`

Soot caught on a cold plate held over a flame. Bound with gum it is ink, which makes it the material Varuna's diary is actually written in. Soot caught on a plate. Nobody gathers it; somebody makes it.

### Leviathan bone

`material_leviathan_bone`

Canon records it as a staple of the far trade. Kept gatherable on the coast as well as at sea because a leviathan bone reaches a beach without the leviathan: it is the one material here that arrives by washing up, which is also the only way a walker on foot ever meets one.

### Lodestone grit

`material_lodestone_grit`

The islands are held up by it. What can be worked loose from an exposed core is a coarse black grit that will not settle in a bowl -- it stands on end and points, which is what a compass is for and why no compass works within a mile of one. Taken from the rock, not from anything living.

### Magma-glass

`material_magma_glass`

Volcanic glass, and canon's Chess of Fate has its squares inlaid with it. Takes an edge sharper than bronze and loses it to a hard look.

### Mahua oil

`material_mahua_oil`

Pressed from the seed. Canon already has the tree down for oil, a fermented drink and a ritual offering, and this is only the first of the three.

### Mango

`material_mango_fruit`

Two crops from one tree a month apart: the green fruit is the sour one cooks with, and the ripe one is not cooked at all.

### Mangrove pole

`material_mangrove_pole`

Cut from the stilt roots rather than the trunk. Already the right shape, already used to standing in salt water, and no use at all for anything that must be straight.

### Mountain tea

`material_mountain_tea`

The young leaves of the wild tea bushes on sulphurous volcanic ground: the dark, sharp black-ash leaf that anyone may pick, and the amber-veined ember leaf the Narmada scholars prize. Plucked at the tip, and the bush flushes again within the season.

### Mustard seed

`material_mustard_seed`

Pressed for a hot yellow oil, or cracked whole into it. A field of mustard is two crops standing in one place.

### Myrrh gum

`material_myrrh_gum`

The wound salve canon names on the myrrh-thorn is this, softened in oil. It is also the best glue in the desert, which is the same property read two ways.

### Native copper

`material_native_copper`

Found as metal rather than smelted from ore -- green-crusted lumps in a dry watercourse. Cold-worked, annealed, worked again.

### Natron

`material_natron`

The bitter salt from a dry lake bed. Cleans, cures hide, and makes glass possible.

### Neem bark

`material_neem_bark`

Canon has the tree for medicine and insect repellent. Bitter enough that nobody takes it for pleasure and reliable enough that everybody has some.

### Oyster shell

`material_oyster_shell`

Shell banks build where the water drops them, so this is taken on a coast as often as from the water the animal lived in.

### Palm husk

`material_palm_husk`

The coarse mat of fibre at the base of a frond. Harsher than reed and far more patient of salt water, which is why the harbour rope is husk and the house rope is reed. The date palms give it in the desert and the palmyra on the dry plains, which is where Dwarka's and the Narmada's comes from.

### Pine resin

`material_pine_resin`

Run from a slash in the bark of the Vindhya pine and scraped off once it has skinned over. Raw, not pitch: pitch is boiled from heartwood in a covered pit, and this is what a traveller can take with a blade and no pot. It lights, and it seals a seam until the sun softens it.

### Pippali

`material_pippali`

Whole spikes picked unripe and dried grey. Hotter than round pepper and traded further.

### Grog

`material_potters_grog`

Old fired pots, crushed and put back into new clay so the new one survives the kiln. The only material in this file that can only be made from itself. Crushed out of old fired pots. There is no grog in the ground.

### Prana-pollen

`material_prana_pollen`

Shaken from the lotus that grows in the inverted falls. Faintly warm in the hand and keeps its warmth for a day after; the cloud-hermits are said to take it for the altitude, though what it treats is not agreed.

### Purified kuchla

`material_purified_kuchla`

What a fortnight of soaking leaves. Still measured in single seeds and still counted out, because the process removes the danger rather than the need for care.

### Red ochre

`material_red_ochre`

Iron-stained earth, ground and washed. The oldest pigment anybody has, and the one every mark in this world is made in until somebody wants blue.

### Reed fibre

`material_reed_fibre`

Retted in standing water until the pith rots away, then combed out and dried. The whole delta runs on it -- matting, basketry, and every rope that does not need to hold a boat.

### River clay

`material_river_clay`

Dug from the cut bank where the water has already done the sorting. The Saraswati's is grey wet and buff fired.

### River fish

`material_river_fish`

Split, salted and dried on a rack. Named for what it is rather than for a species, because a weir does not sort its catch -- so it names the ordinary fish of the river and the estuary together, and not the stepwell koi, which is sacred and is nobody's supper.

### Salt crust

`material_salt_crust`

Scraped from a pan rather than cut from the bush. Kept on the coast as well as the desert because the sea leaves it there without the saltbush having grown: this is the one material canon names a plant for and does not actually require the plant.

### Sandalwood billet

`material_sandalwood_billet`

Heartwood only; the sapwood is scentless and worth nothing. Carved, burnt or ground, and canon has it doing all three.

### Sandstone

`material_sandstone`

Dressed rather than knapped. Soft enough to cut with bronze and hard enough to still be standing when the person who cut it is not.

### Scar glass

`material_scar_glass`

Fused sand from the Glass Scar at Dwarka. Whatever made it is one of the things the player is there to work out, so canon says what it is and pointedly not how it happened.

### Sea salt

`material_sea_salt`

Scraped from the pans the tide fills and the sun empties. Lothal is a harbour on a gulf known for its salt pans, and a fisher there who teaches drying fish is teaching something the shore makes possible. From the ground, not from anything living, so it lies on any coast. Distinct from salt crust, which is the desert's and Dwarka's: that grows on a saltbush and this is left by the sea.

### Shed snakeskin

`material_shed_snakeskin`

A whole shed, jaw to tail, dry as paper and patterned like water over stone. It takes an oiling to be worth anything and then it is the toughest light sheet anybody here has: it does not wet through and it does not crack.

Nothing is taken from a living animal. A serpent sheds when it grows, and the ground where it shed once is the ground it sheds on again -- so this is found by knowing where to look rather than won.

### Shilajit

`material_shilajit`

The tonic canon names on the creeper -- a mineral exudate scraped from high rock. Filed as physic rather than salt because of what it is for, not what it is made of.

### Sinew

`material_sinew`

Filed under hide as well as fibre because of how it is got: skinning, not retting. Dries to a lashing that tightens as it goes, which no plant fibre does.

### Sivatherium milk

`material_sivatherium_milk`

Thick, faintly sweet, and yellow rather than white from the leaves she browses. Taken from a cow that is standing willingly, which is the only way it is taken at all -- a sivatherium that does not want to be milked walks away, and there is no version of this where she is held.

A cow in milk is in milk tomorrow. What is scarce is not the milk but the meeting: she is on one tile of a plateau, and she moves.

### Lodestone moss

`material_sky_moss`

Grows in mats over the ore and takes some of its habit -- a handful pulls very slightly toward iron. Dries to a wadding that holds a dressing in place without a lashing, which is most of why anyone climbs for it.

### Snow moss

`material_snow_moss`

Torn in dense mats off glacier-bound stone. The snow hares line their nests with it, and it does the same for a bedroll or a boot. Slow to come back, which is why a careful hand takes the edge of a mat and not the middle.

### Static sap

`material_static_sap`

Bled from the bramble, and it crackles as it leaves the cut. Sets hard and clear, and a bead of it will lift a hair from the back of your hand across a finger-width of air.

### Tamarind pulp

`material_tamarind_pulp`

Canon files the tree under food, souring agent and shade. This is the first two; the third is not a thing you can carry.

### Lotus-root taro

`material_taro_root`

A flood crop in canon's own words -- it wants the water the rice cannot take, so the two together mean a bad year is survivable.

### Iron-teak timber

`material_teak_timber`

Canon already records this tree for ship timber and tower beams, which is a strong claim about a wood: it means the grain holds under a load that would split anything else growing near it.

### Tin ore

`material_tin_ore`

Cassiterite, heavy and black, panned out of highland gravel. Scarce enough that its trade route is more important than its deposit.

### Pitch

`material_tree_pitch`

Boiled down from resinous heartwood in a covered pit. Nothing floats without it -- every hull in canon is a hull somebody paid this attention. Boiled out of resinous heartwood in a covered pit.

### Viper venom

`material_viper_venom`

Taken from a desert viper held behind the head over the rim of a cup, a few drops at a time, and the animal is let go where it was found. Clear, faintly yellow, and it dries to a varnish. Four serpents of the dry country give it and the caravan people do not much distinguish between them -- what matters is the milking, and a milked viper needs weeks before it is worth milking again.

The only thing it can become is medicine: canon's affordance vocabulary has no word for harm, so the recipes that consume a poison are the ones that purify it -- which is what the tradition it comes from does with a venom.

### Wild honey

`material_wild_honey`

Cut from comb hung on the Narmada cliffs, where the honey-goliath and the siege bee both build. Canon names it medicinal and rich in essential oil; it is also food. The Maru trade it to the asura-blooded for lanterns and saplings, and the upriver expedition logged the cliffside honey-gatherers' pacts without writing down their terms.

### Windfall wood

`material_windfall_wood`

Whatever a tree lets fall: limbs brought down by the monsoon, a trunk the river undercut, the dead lower branches anyone may take. Every tree gives it and none is felled for it, which is why it comes back as fast as weather does. Straight enough for a pole and dry enough for a fire -- the stuff a traveller actually builds and cooks with, where teak and sandalwood are what a city builds with. Added on the owner's ruling of 2 October 2026 that the trunks a traveller walks past should be good for something.

## Items

### Ammonite pendant

`item_ammonite_pendant`

A spiral somebody found already made. The brown ones are worn; the Asura-marked black ones are not worn by anybody sensible.

### Drawn antivenin

`item_antivenin`

Venom cut with neem bark and worked over low heat until it will not kill, which the caravan people have done for longer than anyone here can account for and cannot explain. It is carried by everybody who walks the salt roads and used perhaps twice in a life.

The joke the Tide Market makes of it is that the cure and the poison come off the same animal on the same afternoon, and the only difference is an hour's work.

### Antler pick

`item_antler_pick`

For clay, chalk and soft rock. Free, in the sense that the deer left it lying there.

### Ashwagandha tonic

`item_ashwagandha_tonic`

Root powder in warm oil, taken at night. For exhaustion, which canon distinguishes from illness.

### Bedroll

`item_bedroll`

Oiled cloth over a reed mat, rolled and strapped under the satchel. It has been rained on. **The game has carried this from its first step.** Affords shelter and nothing else: a bedroll strapped under a satchel is a thing being carried, not a thing that carries.

### Blade

`item_blade`

The prototype for anything with a worked edge, before it is told what it is made of.

### Boar spear

`item_boar_spear`

Cross-barred below the head so that what it stops does not walk up it. A defensive object described defensively, which is what canon has.

### Bone awl

`item_bone_awl`

Bores a hole and scores a line. Filed under both cutting and marking because a tally is scratched with the same point that makes a stitch-hole.

### Bone harpoon

`item_bone_harpoon`

The sawfish rostral tooth arrives already barbed, so this is a harpoon somebody did very little to. Canon says what it is for and pointedly not what it does to anything.

### Bow drill

`item_bow_drill`

Makes holes and makes fire, which is the same motion done with different patience.

### Bronze chisel

`item_bronze_chisel`

What dressed sandstone is dressed with. A settlement that has these has a mason.

### Bronze knife

`item_bronze_knife`

Holds an edge, takes a new one, and is worth more than the person carrying it usually is.

### Bronze spearhead

`item_bronze_spearhead`

Socketed, so it outlives its shaft. Traded far past where anybody can make one.

### Bamboomer

`item_cane_chime`

A hand of canes cut to length and bound at the grip, struck against each other, against a saddle, or against whatever is nearest. Each length gives one note and only one, which is why the delta counts an interval in cane rather than in numbers -- two hands of cane is a phrase, and anybody who has walked with a singer knows roughly how far that is.

Tuned by shortening, so it only ever goes up. A set that has been on the road ten years is a set that started lower.

**The name is the cane and the noise in one word**, which is more than the other two manage: a flute is named for what it is made of and a sitar for its shape, and this is named for both at once. Nobody who says it is doing anything as deliberate as a joke -- it is simply what a bamboo does when you hit it, said quickly.

### Carry basket

`item_carry_basket`

Split cane ribs with reed weft. Carries as much as a person can and weighs almost nothing empty.

### Brazier

`item_charcoal_brazier`

Portable fire. Lets a process that needs heat happen somewhere that has none.

### The Fourteen

`item_choddo_shak`

Fourteen greens in one pot, bitter to the last, eaten on one night of the year. Nobody pretends it is pleasant, which is not what it is for.

### Clay seal

`item_clay_seal`

Pressed into the clay closing a jar or a bale. Says who packed it and that nobody has opened it since.

### Cooking pot

`item_cooking_pot`

Coarse-tempered so it survives the fire. Every cooked thing in canon passes through one.

### Cordage

`item_cordage`

The prototype every rope inherits from. Twisted fibre that holds two things together, and the single most useful object in this world by a distance nobody would guess.

### Date block

`item_date_block`

Pressed into a brick and wrapped in leaf. Sweet, heavy, and the reason a desert crossing is arithmetic rather than a gamble.

### Dried fish

`item_dried_fish`

Split, salted, racked. Keeps a season and travels anywhere.

### Fibre cord

`item_fibre_cord`

Two plies of bast or reed rolled on the thigh until they lock. The first lashing anybody makes, and enough to haft an adze or string a bow drill -- it holds, and it has to be retied. Sinew dries tight and does not; that is why a spear or a harpoon still wants sinew, and an adze can start with this.

### Field diary

`item_field_diary`

Bound, and about a third full. **The game's whole progression system**: the crossings-out are the useful part, because a first guess is worth keeping once it is wrong. Affords marking alone. It holds writing, which is not what `contain` or `carry` mean.

### Fire rattle

`item_fire_rattle`

Shells on a line, shaken. Filed as a weapon because that is honestly its category and it is the clearest case in the file that the category is not about harm.

### Fish weir

`item_fish_weir`

A fence in the water that lets the river do the catching. Does not sort what it takes, which is why canon's fish material is named for the river and not for a species.

### Flint knife

`item_flint_knife`

Knapped in an afternoon, sharp for a week, replaced without ceremony.

### Flood bread

`item_flood_bread`

Rice flour stretched with taro, which is what you eat in a year the rice did badly and the flood did well.

### Glass lancet

`item_glass_lancet`

Sharper than bronze and loses the edge to a hard look. Used where the cut matters more than the tool lasting -- surgery, and the finest carving.

### Goat-hair blanket

`item_goat_hair_blanket`

Stays warm wet. On the Vindhya that is the only property anybody asks about.

### Grain bin

`item_grain_bin`

Mud-plastered basketwork, taller than a person. Does not move once filled, which makes it the first object here that only a settlement can own.

### Green mango sour

`item_green_mango_sour`

Unripe mango, salted and dried on a mat. Sour enough to make bad water drinkable, which is what it is carried for.

### Quern

`item_grinding_quern`

Grain to flour, ochre to pigment, bark to powder. The first object written here that had nothing honest to say for itself under the old vocabulary -- which is how `work` came to exist.

### Guggul pill

`item_guggul_pill`

Resin softened and rolled with pepper. Worth carrying a long way, which is most of why the stands are overworked.

### Hafted tool

`item_hafted_tool`

A head bound to a handle. The base for adze, pick and chisel: what they share is the binding, which is why cordage is a prerequisite of half the toolkit.

### Haritaki powder

`item_haritaki_powder`

Dried fruit ground fine. Taken for almost anything, which is usually a sign a remedy does nothing in particular -- canon records the claim without endorsing it.

### Hide tent

`item_hide_tent`

Two people to raise and one to strike. What a caravan sleeps under when there is no travel node within a day.

### Hill curd

`item_hill_curd`

Milk set overnight in a covered pot near the embers, sharp and thick enough to stand a spoon in. The herders carry it wrapped in leaves and eat it with parched grain; it keeps two days in the cold of the plateau and one anywhere else.

A curd is proof that somebody stood beside a wild animal long enough to be given something, and went home and made supper of it.

### Sling

`item_hunting_sling`

Two cords and a pouch. Affords only `deter`: canon records slings keeping birds off a crop far more often than it records them doing anything else.

### Husk hawser

`item_husk_hawser`

Coarse palm-husk line that does not mind salt water. The harbour rope, and the reason a date palm is worth more standing than felled.

### Indigo cloth

`item_indigo_cloth`

Comes out of the vat green and turns blue in the air while you watch. Half its worth is the colour and half is that everyone has seen it happen.

### Ink cake

`item_ink_cake`

Soot bound with gum, dried into a block, wetted on a stone. The substance Varuna's diary is actually written in.

### Green jackfruit pot

`item_jackfruit_curry`

Unripe jackfruit cooked long enough to pull apart in threads. It is not meat and the delta does not claim it is; it is what the tree gives a month before it gives sweets.

### A grain of kuchla

`item_kuchla_grain`

One purified seed ground into oil and divided into doses the size of a grain of rice. For a palsy, and for nothing else -- a preparation this specific is a preparation nobody improvises.

### Loom frame

`item_loom_frame`

Upright, weighted, and taken apart to travel. Weaving needs a thing that works rather than a place that does, and this is that thing.

### Mahua liquor

`item_mahua_liquor`

Canon already records the tree for a fermented drink. Distilled nowhere -- this is the fermented thing itself, and strong enough.

### Mud shoes

`item_mud_shoes`

Splayed frames lashed under the foot. Cross a salt flat or a tidal mud that would otherwise take you to the knee.

### Myrrh poultice

`item_myrrh_poultice`

The wound salve canon names on the myrrh-thorn, softened in oil and spread on bast.

### Neem salve

`item_neem_salve`

Bitter, oily, and keeps a wound clean. Canon has the tree for medicine already.

### Oil lamp

`item_oil_lamp`

A closed clay lamp with a shielded wick, the kind that survives being carried. Throws about an arm of light. **Varuna has carried one since the game's first step** -- canon is recording the kit rather than inventing it.

### Palm-leaf sheet

`item_palm_leaf_sheet`

Cut, boiled, dried and smoothed. Takes a scored line better than it takes ink.

### Prepared food

`item_prepared_food`

The prototype for anything cooked, dried or pressed into a form somebody meant to eat later. A raw tuber and a baked one are different objects and only one is dinner.

### Reed flute

`item_reed_flute`

One length of cane, a notch to blow across and six holes burned rather than cut, because a drilled hole splits the wall and a burned one does not. The cheapest instrument there is and the only one a person makes for themselves on the walk -- an afternoon, and the cane came from the ground they were standing on.

## Processes

### Boatbuilding

`process_boatbuilding`

Hull, frame, lashing and pitch. Sited at settlements and travel nodes because those are the two kinds of place in canon that sit on water and have people.

### Brewing

`process_brewing`

Fermenting. Canon already records the mahua for a fermented drink, so this process is describing something in the record rather than proposing an industry.

### Carving

`process_carving`

Shaping bone, wood, shell or antler with a blade. The most portable of the making processes -- it is what a person does at a camp fire with what they picked up that day.

### Casting

`process_casting`

Molten metal into a mould. Held apart from smelting because the skills are different and the failures are different.

### Cooking

`process_cooking`

Heat and a pot. Deliberately siteless -- a hearth is a fire somebody built, and a traveller builds one wherever they stop.

### Drying and salting

`process_drying`

Preserving by taking the water out. Needs sun, air and patience, none of which canon models, so it needs nothing.

### Firing

`process_firing`

Clay to pottery in a kiln. The first process in this list that a person cannot do alone in a field, and the reason a settlement is worth walking back to.

### Gathering

`process_gathering`

Picking a thing up. Named as a process rather than left implicit because every recipe chain has to start somewhere, and a layer whose raw materials arrive by magic cannot be checked for reachability.

### Grinding

`process_grinding`

Grain to flour, ochre to pigment, bark to powder. One quern does all three and the household that owns it does not lend it -- which is why this is sited: a saddle quern is two heavy stones that live somewhere, and nobody carries one.

### Knapping

`process_knapping`

Striking flakes off stone or glass to leave an edge. Wants nothing but a hammerstone and somewhere to sit, which is why it has no site and no needs.

### Pressing

`process_pressing`

Oil out of seed, under a beam or a stone. Wants a vessel to catch what comes out, which is the whole reason `contain` is a needed affordance and not a nicety.

### Purifying

`process_purifying`

Days of soaking, the liquor thrown away and replaced, and then drying. It is the one process here that adds nothing and removes something -- every other way of making turns a thing into more of a thing, and this turns a poison into a medicine by taking part of it away.

Slow on purpose and impossible to hurry, which is the whole safeguard: a preparation that could be rushed would be rushed.

### Retting

`process_retting`

Rotting the soft parts off a stem in standing water until only fibre is left. Takes days and smells; done at the water's edge and never indoors.

### Smelting

`process_smelting`

Ore to metal. Needs a furnace, charcoal and somebody who has done it before. Canon is a bronze world, so this is near the edge of what anybody can do at all.

### Spinning

`process_spinning`

Drawing fibre into thread on a whorl, and thread into yarn and hawser. The whorl is the tool and the tool is almost always somebody's spare moment. Cord rolled between the palms wants no whorl, and is twisting rather than spinning.

### Tanning

`process_tanning`

Turning hide into leather with bark liquor and time. Sited at a settlement because it needs vats and because nobody will let you do it upwind of them.

### Twisting by hand

`process_twisting`

Rolling fibre into cord between the palms, or down the thigh, with nothing in the hands but the fibre. The oldest making there is, and it needs no tool and no place: it is what a person does with a handful of bast while they wait. Held apart from spinning, which draws thread on a whorl and so wants something that works; a working length of cord does not, and never did.

### Weaving

`process_weaving`

Mat, basket, sail and cloth are one process at four scales. Needs a loom or a frame, which is a thing that works rather than a place that does.

`vehicle_harappan_cart`

The commonest vehicle in canon and the least remarked on, which is the usual fate of the thing everybody has. It is drawn in miniature more often than it is written about -- small clay carts turn up in every settlement, in numbers that make more sense as toys than as votives, though canon does not insist on either.

### Lodestone carriage

`vehicle_lodestone_train`

The line across the Aravali strait does not stand on anything. It is laid on lodestone -- the same Permian-irradiated ore that holds the islands up -- and it hangs at the height the ore wants, which is why the rails run level over open water and why nothing has ever been built under them. Boats pass beneath without lowering a mast.

The carriage is the second half of the same trick: sledges of ore beneath a timber body, riding the repulsion rather than rolling on it. It does not touch the rail and it makes almost no sound, which unsettles people more than the height does.

**One carriage, not a train.** It seats a dozen with their bundles and goes back and forth across the strait, which is the whole of what it does -- the crossing is short and there is nothing at either end that needs moving in quantity. Nobody has coupled a second one to it and nobody has needed to.

It has no bogies. A bogie is the wheeled truck a carriage rides on, and this rides on sledges of lodestone that never touch the rail -- which is why it makes no sound, and why nobody here has a word for the part that would be doing the rolling.

Distinct from the Workshop Train, which canon calls a travelling apothecary rather than transport: that one crosses broken country and is not trying to get anywhere, and it is a train in the sense of a household strung out along a road. This is a box on a rail.

### Dugout

`vehicle_log_dugout`

One trunk, burnt and adzed hollow. The adze blade set across the haft rather than along it is the whole reason this is possible.

### Outrigger

`vehicle_outrigger`

A hull too narrow to stand up on its own, and a float that makes it the steadiest thing on open water. The Kelpfang is one of these, grown rather than built.

### Ox cart

`vehicle_ox_cart`

Solid wheels, no springs, and a pace nobody hurries. Carries what a barge cannot reach.

### Reed raft

`vehicle_reed_raft`

Bundled reed lashed to a cane frame. Rots in a season and is rebuilt in a morning, which is the correct trade for something that only has to cross a channel.

### Salt sled

`vehicle_salt_sled`

Dragged over a salt pan where a wheel would cut through the crust and stop. Dwarka's answer to ground that is neither water nor road.

### The Sinauli wagon

`vehicle_sinauli_wagon`

Copper-sheathed along every edge a hand would touch, with triangular figures worked into the wheel faces. Found only in graves, and only ever with a body in it.

**Canon files it as a cart, and the argument is the point.** Its wheels are solid -- three planks and a copper rim, the same construction as the household cart above -- and a spoked wheel is what would make it a chariot. So the people who bury one are burying something that looks like a war machine and is built like a farm vehicle, and canon does not know which of those they meant. Recording the dispute rather than settling it is the same call made about the Shattering: the question is better than any answer available.

### Workshop train

`vehicle_workshop_train`

A travelling apothecary and workshop rather than transport. The Survival Train gathers the displaced across the broken country, which is a use for a vehicle that has nothing to do with getting anywhere.

## Homesteads

### The pump on the long way

`homestead_dwarka`

Drafted 27 September 2026 for the owner to rewrite. Dwarka's thesis is that local knowledge is simply right, so here the holders are right before the player is: Ushi knows it is not the salt, Jarro knows why the road goes the long way, and both are waiting to see whether the traveller has caught up. A wind-pump rather than a mill, because what Dwarka lacks is sweet water; the glasshouse is a solar still. Drawn with the owner's wind-pump and solar still (27 September 2026). Stages changed 1 October 2026 (Roads and Hands, phase 1) so the homestead can be finished from Dwarka's own ground: the vanes are husk matting rather than bamboo, which barely grows here, and the date palms are offsets the market brings rather than fruit the traveller finds, since Dwarka's desert is small enough that dates are not reliably there.

### The mill among the trees

`homestead_lothal`

Drafted 27 September 2026 for the owner to rewrite. Lothal's thesis is learning to look, and every answer here is something the player has looked at: the salt under the eastern field, the red rice that tolerates it, the winter the camp keeps moving for, the old mill the granary was. Nothing is newly true; it is Bekh's winter line and the red rice, taken to their ends. The Aravali has no homestead by the owner's ruling: it is a crossing.

### The mill at the edge of the survey

`homestead_narmada`

Drafted 27 September 2026 for the owner to rewrite. The Narmada's thesis is a record that begins at the wound, so the University's own steward holds one ground and every worry of hers is the archive's blind spot. The other is the herders' top terrace, where the escarpment wind is the mill's reason -- the same lift the Cloud Stair stands on. Built beside the University, on the owner's ruling. Drawn with the owner's scarp mill, Lothal's glasshouse and the owner's hive (27 September 2026). The tower was lashed with sinew until 1 October 2026; a homestead stage may not wait on a hunt (see AUTHORING.md), so it is lashed with goat-hair cord, which the plateau's herders have in plenty.
